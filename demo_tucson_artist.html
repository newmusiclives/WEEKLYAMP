From the Editor

Welcome to the November 2 issue of the Tucson Artist Edition. Tucson punches well above its weight as a working music city — downtown has been a hub since the 1980s, a former monastery chapel just turned one year old as a concert hall, and the folk festival that built this scene has already posted its 2027 dates. This issue is your field guide for the weeks ahead: what’s open and booking, what’s on the calendar this month, and which deadlines are live.

One thing we want to flag at the top: the Tucson studio landscape has changed in the past few years and we are still being deliberately careful about which rooms we name. We have not yet verified a current studio shortlist, so this issue doesn’t publish one. Every venue below was re-checked against a current calendar or listing this week.

If you've been here twenty years, tell us what we got wrong. Reply to this email and we'll fix it.

— Sarah Collins, Editor, Artist Edition

THE TUCSON SOUND, ON THE RECORD

You can argue about whether there is one “Tucson sound” or several — but it's hard to argue with the credit list of one engineer who has shaped a remarkable share of records made here. Craig Schumacher, working out of Tucson for decades, has recorded and mixed albums for Calexico, Devotchka, Neko Case, Giant Sand, The Jayhawks, Friends of Dean Martinez, and Amos Lee, among others.

That credit list does a lot of the genre-defining work for us. It also tells you something about why Tucson keeps producing the kind of records that land on year-end lists in cities much bigger than this one. The studios change. The talent stays.

What you should take from this if you're writing songs in Tucson in 2026: the audience here grew up on these records. They don't need you to chase the sound. They will hear whatever you make through the lens of a city that already absorbed Calexico and Neko Case. That's a gift, not a constraint.

TUCSON VENUES

Tucson's venue ecosystem isn't huge — but the ones that book original music are clustered downtown and on 4th Avenue, which makes the city much easier to navigate than its size suggests. Here's the field guide for November 2026, re-checked October 30.

Club Congress (311 E Congress St) — At Hotel Congress in downtown Tucson since 1985. Multi-stage live music venue and cultural hub. Books national touring acts and local originals almost every night. Public calendar at hotelcongress.com/calendar. Tonight (Monday, November 2): First Monday Open Mic returns to the Tap Patio at Hotel Congress — doors 6:30pm, show 7pm, free, 21+, hosted by JC Chiriguayo, with a special set from Tucson’s Romo Lampkin. Bring a song.

191 Toole (191 E Toole Ave) — Sister venue to the Rialto Theatre. Capacity 500. The room you graduate to once you've drawn at the smaller downtown clubs. Phone (520) 740-1000. Booking actively this month — Two Runner plays Thursday, November 12.

Rialto Theatre (318 E Congress St) — Historic downtown concert hall. National touring acts, with local openers booked for genre-matched headliners. The Rialto also manages 191 Toole bookings, which means there is a real path through the room. November is packed: Paul Cauthen (Nov. 3), Rodrigo y Gabriela (Nov. 8), and two nights of Brandi Carlile (Nov. 9 and 10) are on the Live Nation calendar.

Solar Culture Gallery (31 E Toole Ave) — All-ages art gallery and performance space in a historic art-deco building, running since 1987. Wooden dance floor, full stage, real lights, real PA. The vibe is closer to a respected DIY space than a club. If you make music that doesn't fit Congress, this is probably your room. Its current schedule runs through mid-November, though the two dated events posted (Nov. 5 and Nov. 15) are a breathwork session and a voice workshop rather than concerts — contact the gallery directly about show bookings.

The Hut (305 N 4th Ave) — 4th Avenue tiki bar with a 45-foot Easter Island Tiki Head out front. Original live music in the Arts District between University and downtown. Tropical drinks, large patio. Closed Mon-Tue. Wed-Fri 5pm-2am, Sat 2pm-2am, Sun 2pm-midnight (the Sunday close is an hour earlier than the listing showed in May). (520) 623-3200.

La Rosa (800 N Country Club Rd) — New to this guide. The former Benedictine Monastery chapel in midtown, converted into a concert venue by David Slutes (formerly music director at Hotel Congress) and Phoenix entertainment veteran Charlie Levy. Its opening show on October 10, 2025 sold out with 750 people. A year in, the calendar is dense and mixes touring names with Tucson bands: XIXA was booked for October 30, a Rock the Old Pueblo: Battle of the Bands was scheduled for Halloween morning, and Orkesta Mendoza plays November 27.

A note on Exo Roast Co. We listed Exo’s evening music in May. This week we could confirm the business is trading but could not confirm a current live-music schedule, so we’ve left it out of the field guide until we can.

The realistic Tucson playbook: start at an open mic like tonight’s, or at Solar Culture or The Hut, build to Club Congress, and work toward 191 Toole, La Rosa, or a Rialto opener slot.

A NOTE ON RECORDING IN TUCSON (2026)

For two decades, the answer to “where do I record in Tucson?” ran through the downtown room where Craig Schumacher tracked records for the artists listed up top — and that room appears to have wound down. Six months on, we still have not verified a replacement shortlist, so we are still not going to publish one.

What we will say is this: the engineers who built that catalogue still live in this city. Working Tucson musicians know who is taking sessions in 2026, and the path to finding them runs through the venues, not directories. Show up at Club Congress on a weeknight. Go to a Solar Culture set and stay through the openers. Ask the bands who they tracked with. The studio scene that comes out of those conversations will be more current than anything we could publish today.

Issue #2 will carry a re-verified studio guide with rates, contacts, and at least three sourced credits per room.

THE FESTIVAL THAT BUILT THIS SCENE

If you want to understand why Tucson punches up as a music city, start with the Tucson Folk Festival. The 2027 festival is set for April 2-4, 2027, and the homepage bills it as “42 Years Running.” This year’s edition (April 10-12, 2026, at Jácome Plaza downtown, presented by the Tucson Kitchen Musicians Association) was free and all-ages, with six stages, more than 150 performances, a young-artist showcase, and a songwriting competition.

The deadline that matters now: the homepage says “Performer and vendor applications are open!” The performer-application page itself hasn’t been updated for 2027 yet — it still describes the 2026 festival. Its terms: no fee through October 31, a $30 late fee from November 1, and a hard close at 11:59pm on November 15. If those terms carry over to this cycle, the free window just closed and you have until November 15. Check the form before you count on that. Performer slots are unpaid volunteer slots (headliners are invited separately), the focus is “acoustic-oriented folk and related styles,” you submit three MP3s, and you don’t need to be a TKMA member. The songwriting competition has its own application.

That kind of infrastructure doesn't come from city budgets. TKMA is a working musician's organization, and the festival has run continuously for four decades because Tucson's music community shows up to make it run. Performer applications, vendor info, and the full schedule live at tucsonfolkfest.org.

For a Tucson artist looking for a meaningful first festival, this is it. The barrier to entry is the application process, not a booking-agency relationship, and the songwriting competition is a real path to broader exposure for unsigned writers.

Also on the calendar. The 37th annual All Souls Procession weekend runs Friday, November 6 through Sunday, November 8, 2026. The organizers haven’t posted a call for musicians, but they are recruiting volunteers. The Tucson Jazz Festival runs January 15-23, 2027 (Trombone Shorty and Orleans Avenue at the Fox Tucson Theatre on January 23), with a Ron Carter encore at the Rialto on January 28.

A grant you just missed — note it for next year. The Arts Foundation for Tucson and Southern Arizona’s 2027 stART Grant (up to $5,000 for individual artists and 501(c)3s in Southern Arizona, with Community Impact and Artist Development tracks) closed October 6, 2026. Questions go to grantprogram@artsfoundtucson.org.
