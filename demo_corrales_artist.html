From the Editor

Welcome to Issue #1 of the Corrales Artist Edition. When we floated the idea of doing a hyperlocal newsletter for a village of 8,500 people sandwiched between Albuquerque and Rio Rancho, the room split. Half the team thought it was crazy. The other half had spent enough Saturday nights at Casa Vieja's patio to know better. Corrales is small. The scene that hangs around it is not.

This issue is your field guide for the indoor season. The patios are emptying, the village council has just put decibel numbers on paper for next spring, and the metro's best listening room has just launched a $5.2 million campaign to rebuild itself. We'll walk you through the venues that matter (here and across the river in Albuquerque), the studios within an hour's drive, and what's on the calendar after today. We are being careful about names: every venue, studio, and event below was checked against a current source this week, and the sources sit at the foot of each section. Where we couldn't confirm something, we say so. If anything looks wrong, write us. We'll fix it in Issue #2.

— Sarah Collins, Editor, Artist Edition

WRITING IN THE BOSQUE

November is when Corrales music moves indoors, and this year the move comes with homework. On October 27 the village council discussed a fourth draft of a new noise ordinance. It is not law yet. But it is the first version with real numbers in it, and if you play patios on Corrales Road, it is worth knowing before next spring.

The short version: limits are measured on the neighbour's property, not at your amp. Next to residential land, the draft allows 55 dBA in the daytime and 45 at night, plus a separate bass-weighted limit (60 dBC daytime). “Daytime” ends at 10pm on Fridays and Saturdays and at 9pm the rest of the week. After that, amplified music that is plainly audible beyond the property line can count as a violation with no meter reading at all. The village wants the new rules in place before the spring patio season. A revised draft, a public hearing and a final vote all come first, and no hearing date has been set.

What that means for your writing desk is practical. A patio set that works at 55 dBA at the neighbour's fence is a quieter, more acoustic set than a lot of bands carry. If you have one, rehearse it this winter. If you don't, the rooms that keep booking patios next year will be asking for it.

One more indoor-season note: the Corrales Arts Center has moved into a storefront at 4436 Corrales Rd, Suite A1 (once occupied by Casa Vieja). There is a members-only opening on November 13 and a public open house on Sunday, November 15, 1–5pm, with a silent auction of work donated by local artists. President Charlene Spiegel told the Comment, “You don't have to live in Corrales (to be a member).” Membership is open to people who aren't artists, too. The coverage mentions classes and activities, not concerts, so treat it as a place to meet the village's working artists rather than a gig.

CORRALES & NORTH VALLEY VENUES

Corrales itself is a village. Inside village limits, a handful of rooms host original music, and in November most of that music is indoors or in daylight. For everything else, you cross the river or take Coors south — the whole metro is about twenty-five minutes away.

Casa Vieja (4541 Corrales Rd) — Still the center of gravity for live music in the village. The building is a 1770 adobe; the brewery inside says it features “local musicians and food trucks almost every day,” on the patio or inside when the weather turns. Closed Mondays and Tuesdays; open from 4pm Wednesday to Friday and mid-afternoon on weekends. The online calendar showed no dated listings when we checked, so the brewery's social pages are the live schedule. Walk in on a quiet weeknight, talk to the staff, ask how to get on the calendar.

Sandia Bar (4445 Corrales Rd) — The 1948 bar that reopened with a patio and a rooftop. It has free live music Saturdays and Sundays, 2–5pm, listed as a family-friendly daytime slot. Pitches go through the “Play Music at Sandia Bar?” form on its site; pay, PA and set length aren't published, so ask. We couldn't confirm whether the weekend slot runs through the winter.

Indigo Crow Cafe (4515 Corrales Rd) — Contemporary American restaurant that's been in the village 25+ years. Its live music is on the patio, mostly on summer evenings, so it's one to pitch in spring, not now. Closed Mondays. Phone (505) 898-7000.

Historic Old San Ysidro Church (966 Old Church Rd) — Main home of the Music in Corrales series, now in its 40th-anniversary season. The series is presented by the volunteer Corrales Cultural Arts Council, which created it in 1986. Be realistic: this season's lineup is touring classical, jazz and traditional acts, and the series publishes no artist submission process. It's a room to hear the standard, not a cold pitch.

Outpost Performance Space (210 Yale Blvd SE, Albuquerque) — The serious listening room of the metro: non-profit, member-supported, smoke and alcohol free, with a jazz, world, contemporary-classical and songwriter focus. On October 19 it launched a $5.2 million campaign, with $2.5 million already raised. The money is for renovating the building, a new Kaman Music Education Center for K–12 classes, an expanded outdoor courtyard and an endowment named for founder Tom Guralnick. No construction dates have been announced. To pitch a show, Outpost's booking page asks for a publicity packet mailed to Outpost Productions, PO Box 4543, Albuquerque, NM 87196, or ideas emailed to the director.

Sister Bar (407 Central Ave NW, Albuquerque) — A 360-capacity downtown room with a diverse curated calendar: national touring acts, locals, dance nights. Booking: events@sisterthebar.com. Live music plus a full bar and 37 beers on draught.

Launchpad (618 Central Ave SW, Albuquerque) — Music hall on Central for national acts and locals. We've dropped the opening hours we printed last time; they no longer match the venue's ticket listings, so check the show page.

Tractor Brewing Wells Park (1800 4th St NW, Albuquerque) — The original Tractor taproom. Hosts live music, karaoke, art shows and community events, with a patio. Open every day. (505) 243-6752.

Tumbleroot Pottery Pub (135 W Palace Ave, Santa Fe) — An hour up I-25, just off the Plaza, open daily 11am–9pm. Sister venue to Tumbleroot Brewery & Distillery. We previously listed a Wednesday open mic and nightly summer patio music here; neither is on the pub's site now, and we couldn't reconfirm them, so we've taken them out.

The working playbook for a new Corrales artist hasn't changed: start with a Casa Vieja weeknight or a Sandia Bar weekend afternoon, build to Tractor or Sister Bar, and send Outpost a packet once you have a focused set worth a listening room.

RECORDING WITHIN AN HOUR

Corrales doesn't have a commercial studio district. What it has is a stack of working rooms an hour up the road in Santa Fe, plus a steady rotation of project studios in the metro that you'll find through working musicians rather than directories. The verified options are below.

Frogville Studios (Santa Fe)

At 111 Calle Nopal. “Established officially in 2003.” The studio's page describes a full set of tracking rooms: control room, amp room, isolation booth and a big room. Worth the drive once you have a project that justifies the rate. Rates aren't published; booking and information at frogvilleplanet.com/recording-studio.

The Kitchen Sink (528 Jose St, Santa Fe)

Music production studio in Santa Fe offering recording, mixing and mastering, with a tracking room and a large instrument collection. Its site lists work with John Popper of Blues Traveler, Crystal Bowersox, KT Tunstall, Terry Allen and Robert Mirabal, among others. Information at thekitchensinkstudio.com.

Stepbridge Studios (528 Jose St, Santa Fe)

In the same building as The Kitchen Sink, but a different shop. Stepbridge is a post-production room — ADR, voice-over, audiobook recording. Excellent at what it does. Not the place to track a band. Listed here so you don't waste a phone call: stepbridge.com.

Project rooms in the metro. Several of the metro's best engineers run home rigs out of casitas and converted garages. We are still not listing any of them, because we have not been able to verify current rates and availability. If you want a recommendation, talk to the regulars at Casa Vieja and Tractor — they will know who is taking sessions this month.

TOURING OUT OF THE METRO

Touring out of Corrales is its own discipline. The geography here punishes bad planning — it's six hours to Denver, four to El Paso, six to Phoenix, and the Texas Panhandle is a long flat slog before a paying gig. Here are three loops worth sketching, with drive time in mind. (We are listing only the cities and the broad routes here. Specific out-of-state venues will land in a future issue once we've verified current bookers.)

The Front Range run — Albuquerque to Santa Fe to Taos to Pueblo to Colorado Springs to Denver, back via Trinidad and Las Vegas, NM. Short drives between stops, and a lot of high country. In winter this is the loop that most needs a weather day.

The border loop — Albuquerque to Las Cruces to El Paso to Marfa, back via Silver City and Truth or Consequences. Lower and warmer than the Front Range, which makes it the easier winter sketch.

The I-40 west push — Albuquerque to Gallup to Flagstaff to Sedona to Phoenix to Tucson, back. Long stretches between gigs. In November, the low-desert end of this loop is the warm-weather option; the high-country legs are not.

Three things to plan around this winter:

  • Altitude is the winter variable. Flagstaff, Raton Pass on I-25, and the Front Range all sit high. Between now and spring, build a weather day into any route that crosses them, and check state road conditions before you load out.
  • The I-25 corridor is your friend. El Paso to Denver is a single interstate. You can chain markets without expensive detours.
  • Santa Fe is the cheapest out-of-town date you have. It's an hour up I-25, close enough to play a show and be home in your own bed the same night. Tumbleroot Pottery Pub is open daily off the Plaza; ask about their music nights directly, since we couldn't confirm a current schedule.

The reality of touring out of Corrales is that you'll burn more gas than bands based in major hubs. Winter is the season to book spring routes, not to drive them.

FESTIVALS & OPEN MICS

The festival season is over. What's left on the calendar after today is listening-room concerts, community events and one grant deadline. Here's what we could confirm.

Music in Corrales — the 40th-anniversary season. Coming dates from the series' own listings:

  • Sat, Nov 14, 6pm — Rachel Barton Pine, solo classical violin. $32 advance, $35 door, $20 students. Heads up: the venue is listed two ways. The series' ticket page says Rio Rancho High School (301 Loma Colorado Blvd NE); its other listings say Cibola High School Performing Arts Center. Check your ticket before you drive.
  • Wed, Dec 16, 6pm — Kenari Quartet (saxophone), Old San Ysidro Church. Listed as sold out.
  • Sat, Jan 23, 2027, 6pm — Natasha Paremski, classical piano, Old San Ysidro Church.

Corrales Starlight Parade — Sat, Dec 5. The 23rd annual parade, sponsored by Corrales MainStreet, runs from Wagner's parking lot south on Corrales Road. The road closes between Tenorio Rd and Coronado Rd from 5:15 to 6:30pm. It's a village holiday event, not a paid gig, but if you're booked anywhere on Corrales Road that night, plan your load-in around the closure.

Outpost Record Fair — Sat, Nov 21, 9am–2pm. This year it's a benefit sale for Outpost, Chatter and Opera Southwest, held at Chatter, 912 3rd Street NW.

Globalquerque! — New Mexico's annual world-music festival is a September event. We couldn't confirm a 2026 edition. The most recent one we could document was September 20, 2025 at Civic Plaza. We'll report the 2027 dates and submission window once they're posted.

One deadline: New Mexico Arts FY28 grants. These are fixed $5,000 awards, up to about 200 of them, with a final online deadline of 11:59pm MT, December 18, 2026. The guidelines also print December 8 in one checklist line, so file early. The catch for musicians: individual artists can't apply. Applicants have to be nonprofits, schools, government or tribal entities, or a group working through a 501(c)(3) fiscal agent. And if you're a new applicant, the advance-review deadline already passed on October 23. If your band works with a nonprofit presenter, this is the conversation to have with them this month.

Open mics: we couldn't confirm a current weekly open mic anywhere in this issue's radius. The Tumbleroot Wednesday night we listed before is no longer on the pub's site, and Tractor Wells Park's calendar shows karaoke and themed nights but no open mic. If you run or regularly play one in Corrales or the North Valley, send us a link we can read and we'll list it in Issue #2.

🎧 Trivia Answer

B) Corrales Cultural Arts Council

Per the series' own site: “The all-volunteer Corrales Cultural Arts Council (CCAC) created the Music in Corrales series in 1986.” Most of the concerts happen at the Historic Old San Ysidro Church, which is why people often credit the Historical Society. The 2026–27 season is billed as the 40th anniversary.
