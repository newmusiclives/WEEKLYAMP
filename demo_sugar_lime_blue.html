From the Editor

Welcome to Issue #1 of the Sugar Lime Blue artist edition. This is the first of our artist-specific dispatches, and we picked Sugar Lime Blue on purpose: a Tennessee band whose history reads like a working musician's case study in slow growth, real partnerships, and refusing to give up after a hard loss. Inside: the band's verified history, who's on stage in 2026, the full discography (now with release dates), and an honest answer to “when can I see them next?” — which, as of this week, is “no date is posted yet.” Every fact comes with a source link.

WHO THEY ARE

Sugar Lime Blue are an Americana / blues / folk / country / jam group based in Nashville, Tennessee. The band self-describes their style as “Cosmic Cowgirl” — a phrase that does more useful work than most genre tags, because it tells you to expect Grateful-Dead-shaped patience underneath blues phrasing and country instrumentation.

The band's roots go back to 1999, when guitarist Dave Beth and vocalist Ashley Beth first met in Arlington, Texas. Ashley had been asked to add background vocals to Dave's earlier project. They married, moved to Wilson County, Tennessee in 2004, kept writing together, and gave Sugar Lime Blue its first official shows in 2007. The band name itself was Dave's coinage at home; in Ashley's preferred description: “Sugar, the sweet stuff; Lime, the citrus fruit; Blue, the color.” Sweet, sour, and bluesy.

THE LINEUP, NOW

In 2026, Sugar Lime Blue has mostly been a two-piece on stage. Of the 2026 dates on the band's ReverbNation calendar (February through September), the large majority are billed as the SLB Acoustic Duo — Ashley and Dave — with only a handful billed as full band (among them White Squirrel Brewery in Bowling Green, KY on April 4 and July 17, and the Mother's Day town concert in Sanford, NC on May 10).

  • Ashley Beth — lead vocals. Co-founder.
  • Dave Beth — lead guitar. Co-founder.

For the full-band configuration, the band's Bandsintown profile listed Matt Fox (bass) and Ikaika Pekelo (drums and percussion) when we checked in May 2026. We could not reload that page this week and found no second source naming the current rhythm section, so treat that as last-confirmed in May rather than confirmed today.

Two members of the band's earlier eras deserve naming. Bassist Russ Dean joined around 2010, after relocating to the Nashville area from California, and the trio that formed around the Beths leaned hard on a shared love of jam-band music — the Grateful Dead in particular. Russ Dean passed away in 2020. After that loss, Joe Bass (also performing as Brian Howe) carried the bass chair on tour through the band's interim period.

That history matters because Sugar Lime Blue's catalogue is shaped by it. The records before and after 2020 are the same band, but they are not the same year of that band's life.

THE DISCOGRAPHY, ON THE RECORD

Four albums across eleven years and two studios — one their own, one of Nashville's most storied rooms. We've now filled in the two release dates we left blank in May.

Far From The Tree (2011) — Debut album, 12 tracks. Released independently.

Move That Earth (2015) — Sophomore album, 7 tracks. Bandcamp dates the release August 1, 2015; The Wilson Post reported at the time that the CD came out that July and that a 300-copy vinyl run was fan-funded through Kickstarter, covering about 90 percent of production costs.

Narcoluptuous (2019) — The third studio album, 12 tracks, released May 15, 2019 per Bandcamp. The Bandcamp credits read “Recorded at Bluebird Studios, Lebanon TN” — Bluebird being the band's own home studio — and “Mastered by Jim Haydon Complex One.”

The Blackbird Sessions (2022) — Still the latest album: 9 tracks, recorded at the iconic Blackbird Studio in Nashville. Indie Band Guru highlighted “Burn It” and “Didn't Mean To Kill You” as standouts; the album was picked up by more than 100 FM radio stations on release. On its own site the band says the “Burn It” video has passed 100K YouTube plays and that “Hello October” has been used in more than 60K Instagram reels — their figures, which we haven't independently checked. The record arrives on the other side of Russ Dean's passing, which gives some of its quieter moments their full weight.

We searched for a newer release — single, EP, or album — and found nothing dated after 2022. If one is coming, it hasn't been announced anywhere we could find.

WHERE TO CATCH THEM

Upcoming shows: none confirmed. As of October 30, 2026 we could not find a single Sugar Lime Blue date after November 2 on any current source. Here's what we checked:

  • sugarlimeblue.com/tickets — the band's own ticketing page. Its most recent listing is the Artist Collective #002 event at FX Pedals in Lebanon, TN on December 2, 2025. Nothing newer.
  • ReverbNation events calendar — the most complete 2026 record we found. The latest date on it is September 26, 2026. Nothing after.
  • Songkick — zero upcoming events. JamBase — “no upcoming Sugar Lime Blue tour dates.” 3rd and Lindsley’s artist page — “There are currently no upcoming events.”
  • Bandsintown — we couldn't load it this week, so it's the one source we couldn't rule out. If a new date appears, it will probably show up there first.

Search results also turned up December 18 (Chattanooga, TN) and December 19 (Dahlonega, GA) duo dates with no year attached. The Dahlonega venue, Canopy and the Roots, lists that show as December 19, 2025, so these are last year's dates and we have not listed them as upcoming.

How 2026 went, on the record (all past): a dense spring and summer of regional duo dates around Tennessee and Kentucky; a Summer '26 run west that took in Little Toad Creek Brewery & Distillery in Silver City, NM (June 21) and Spearfish Public House in Spearfish, SD (June 27); a Northeast swing in early September (The Buttonball Barn in South Egremont, MA on Sept. 3; The Loft 129 in Cumberland, MD on Sept. 4; Kennett Flash in Kennett Square, PA on Sept. 5; Tennessee Ave. Beer Hall in Atlantic City, NJ on Sept. 6); then The Italian Bistro in Burkesville, KY (Sept. 19) and The Gaslight in Huntingburg, IN (Sept. 26), the last listed date. The rooms they came back to most this year were The Crusty Pig in Hopkinsville, KY, The White Squirrel in Bowling Green, KY, The Italian Bistro in Burkesville, KY, and Tenn Lakes Brewing in Lebanon, TN. That's history, not a forecast, but if you want to watch a calendar, those are the ones.

One correction to our May fact sheet: we had the Bryan Symphony Orchestra fundraiser in Cookeville, TN down as May 6, 2026. The current listing dates it May 13, 2026. We've fixed the sheet.

WHAT WE DIDN'T INCLUDE, AND WHY

A note about the things this issue does not say.

Upcoming shows. We list none because we could confirm none. Old dates get recycled as “upcoming” on aggregator pages all the time; we'd rather print an empty calendar than send you to a show that already happened.

The full-band rhythm section. The bass and drums names above were last confirmed in May 2026. We're keeping them because they were sourced then, and flagging them because we couldn't reconfirm them now.

Labels. The band has operated independently across the catalogue. If a release was distributed through a third-party imprint, we want a source before we name one.

We also do not name a touring booker, a press contact, or a current management team. Those relationships change. We'll add them as we confirm them.

If you have current information on any of the above — a new date, a credit, a new release — reply to this email with a source link. We update the verified facts sheet weekly.

🎧 Trivia Answer

B) Blackbird Studio in Nashville

The Blackbird Sessions (2022) was tracked at the iconic Blackbird Studio in Nashville — nine songs, picked up by 100+ FM radio stations on release. The band's home studio “Bluebird” in Lebanon, TN, east of Nashville, was the room behind the 2019 Narcoluptuous record. Both spellings are easy to confuse and we have done it ourselves.
